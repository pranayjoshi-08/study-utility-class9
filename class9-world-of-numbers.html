🔢 The World of Numbers

📖 Chapter Overview

Numbers are used to count, measure, compare and describe quantities. In this chapter, we explore different kinds of numbers and the relationships between them.

🔢 Different Types of Numbers

Natural Numbers

1, 2, 3, 4, 5, ... are called natural numbers.

Whole Numbers

0, 1, 2, 3, 4, ... are whole numbers.

Integers

..., -3, -2, -1, 0, 1, 2, 3, ... are integers.

Rational Numbers

A rational number can be written in the form p/q, where p and q are integers and q ≠ 0.

Examples: 1/2, -3/4, 5, 0.25

Irrational Numbers

Numbers that cannot be written in the form p/q are called irrational numbers.

Examples: √2, √3, √5

Real Numbers

Rational and irrational numbers together form the real numbers.

⭐ Number Line

A number line is a straight line on which numbers are represented at equal intervals.

Positive numbers lie to the right of zero and negative numbers lie to the left of zero.

Example: -3 < -2 < -1 < 0 < 1 < 2 < 3

🧠 Comparing Numbers

On a number line, the number farther to the right is greater than the number farther to the left.

Example: 5 > 2

Also: -2 > -5

➕ Properties of Numbers

Closure Property

Adding two integers always gives another integer.

Example: 3 + (-5) = -2

Commutative Property

a + b = b + a

Example: 3 + 5 = 5 + 3

Associative Property

(a + b) + c = a + (b + c)

Distributive Property

a(b + c) = ab + ac

✏️ Solved Examples

Example 1

Which is greater: -3 or -7?

Show Answer

On the number line, -3 lies to the right of -7.

✅ Therefore, -3 > -7.

Example 2

Is 3/5 a rational number?

Show Answer

Yes. It is already in the form p/q where q ≠ 0.

✅ Therefore, 3/5 is rational.

Example 3

Simplify: 3(4 + 2)

Show Solution

3(4 + 2) = 3 × 4 + 3 × 2

= 12 + 6

✅ Answer = 18
