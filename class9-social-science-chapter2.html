📖 Chapter Overview

Earth's surface is constantly changing. Forces acting inside and outside the Earth shape mountains, valleys, plains and other landforms.

In this chapter, we learn how internal and external processes shape the Earth's surface.

🌋 Earth's Internal Forces

Forces that originate inside the Earth are called internal or endogenic forces.

These forces can cause earthquakes, volcanic activity, folding and faulting of rocks.

🌎 Earth's Plates

The outer rigid layer of Earth is divided into large pieces called tectonic plates.

These plates move slowly over the underlying part of Earth's interior.

Important Terms

  • Lithosphere – rigid outer layer of Earth.
  • Asthenosphere – relatively softer layer beneath the lithosphere.
  • Tectonic plates – large moving sections of the lithosphere.

↔️ Plate Boundaries

The places where tectonic plates meet are called plate boundaries.

1. Convergent Boundary

Two plates move towards each other.

2. Divergent Boundary

Two plates move away from each other.

3. Transform Boundary

Two plates slide past each other.

🌋 Volcanoes

A volcano is an opening in Earth's crust through which molten material, gases and other volcanic products may reach the surface.

Volcanic activity is closely related to the movement and interaction of tectonic plates.

🌐 Earthquakes

An earthquake is the shaking of the Earth's surface caused by the sudden release of energy within the Earth.

Earthquakes are commonly associated with movements along faults and tectonic plate boundaries.

🌧️ External Forces

Forces acting on the Earth's surface from outside are called external or exogenic forces.

Weathering, erosion and deposition are important processes that reshape the surface.

🪨 Weathering

Weathering is the breaking down or alteration of rocks at or near the Earth's surface.

Types of Weathering

  • Physical weathering
  • Chemical weathering
  • Biological weathering

🌊 Erosion

Erosion is the wearing away and removal of weathered material by agents such as running water, wind, glaciers and waves.

🏔️ Deposition

Deposition occurs when transported material is dropped or deposited in a new location.

Erosion and deposition together help create many different landforms.

💧 Running Water

Rivers and streams can erode rocks and soil and transport sediments from one place to another.

Over time, running water can form valleys, gorges, floodplains and other landforms.

❄️ Glaciers

Glaciers are large masses of moving ice. They can erode and transport rocks and sediments.

Glacial activity can create distinctive mountain landforms and valleys.

💨 Wind

Wind can transport and deposit fine particles, especially in dry regions.

Wind erosion and deposition can form features such as sand dunes.

💧 Groundwater

Groundwater can dissolve certain types of rocks and contribute to the formation of underground features.

🏞️ Landforms

Landforms are natural features of the Earth's surface. They are shaped by both internal and external processes.

  • Mountains
  • Plateaus
  • Plains
  • Valleys
  • Gorges
  • Sand dunes

⚠️ Natural Hazards

Some geological processes can create hazards for people. Understanding these processes helps communities prepare for and reduce their effects.

  • Earthquakes
  • Volcanic eruptions
  • Landslides
  • Glacial Lake Outburst Floods (GLOFs)
  • Avalanches

⭐ Quick Revision

  • Internal forces originate inside the Earth.
  • Tectonic plates are large moving sections of the lithosphere.
  • Plate boundaries may be convergent, divergent or transform.
  • Earthquakes result from sudden releases of energy within the Earth.
  • Volcanoes are associated with Earth's internal processes.
  • External forces reshape Earth's surface.
  • Weathering breaks down or alters rocks.
  • Erosion removes and transports material.
  • Deposition occurs when transported material is laid down.
  • Water, wind and glaciers are important agents of erosion and deposition.
