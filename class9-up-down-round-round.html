⭕ I'm Up and Down, and Round and Round

Class 9 Mathematics — Ganita Manjari

📖 Chapter Overview

This chapter explores the fascinating geometry of circles. We study symmetry, chords, arcs, angles, circumcircles and cyclic quadrilaterals.

⭕ Basic Terms

Circle

A circle is the set of all points in a plane that are at the same distance from a fixed point called the centre.

Centre

The fixed point from which every point on the circle is at the same distance.

Radius

The distance from the centre to any point on the circle.

Chord

A line segment joining any two points on a circle.

Diameter

A chord passing through the centre of the circle. It is the longest chord.

Diameter = 2 × Radius

Arc

An arc is a part of the circumference between two points on a circle.

🔄 Symmetry of a Circle

A circle has reflection symmetry across every diameter.

A circle also has rotational symmetry about its centre.

  • Every diameter is a line of reflection symmetry.
  • A circle looks unchanged when rotated about its centre.

🎯 How Many Circles?

Through one point, infinitely many circles can be drawn.

Through two distinct points, infinitely many circles can also be drawn. Their centres lie on the perpendicular bisector of the line segment joining the two points.

Through three non-collinear points, exactly one circle can be drawn. This is called the circumcircle.

🔺 Circumcircle and Circumcentre

The circle passing through all three vertices of a triangle is called its circumcircle.

The centre of this circle is called the circumcentre.

  • For an acute triangle, the circumcentre lies inside.
  • For an obtuse triangle, the circumcentre lies outside.
  • For a right triangle, the circumcentre is the midpoint of the hypotenuse.

📏 Chords and Angles

Equal chords of a circle subtend equal angles at the centre.

Conversely, chords that subtend equal angles at the centre are equal in length.

📐 Perpendicular from Centre to a Chord

The line joining the centre to the midpoint of a chord is perpendicular to the chord.

Also, the perpendicular from the centre to a chord bisects the chord.

📍 Distance of Chords from Centre

Equal chords are at equal distances from the centre.

Conversely, chords that are at equal distances from the centre are equal in length.

The longer chord is closer to the centre.

🌙 Angles Subtended by Arcs

The angle subtended by an arc at the centre is twice the angle subtended by the same arc at any point on the remaining part of the circle.

Therefore:

Central angle = 2 × angle at the circle

Angle in a Semicircle

The angle subtended by a diameter at any point on the circle is always:

90°

🔗 Concyclic Points

Four or more points are called concyclic if they all lie on the same circle.

If a line segment joining two points subtends equal angles at two other points on the same side, the four points are concyclic.

🔷 Cyclic Quadrilateral

A quadrilateral whose four vertices lie on the same circle is called a cyclic quadrilateral.

The opposite angles of a cyclic quadrilateral are supplementary.

Opposite angle 1 + Opposite angle 2 = 180°

Conversely, if a pair of opposite angles of a quadrilateral adds up to 180°, the quadrilateral is cyclic.

⭐ Important Theorems

  1. A unique circle passes through three non-collinear points.
  2. Equal chords subtend equal angles at the centre.
  3. Chords subtending equal central angles are equal.
  4. The line from the centre to the midpoint of a chord is perpendicular to the chord.
  5. The perpendicular from the centre to a chord bisects the chord.
  6. Equal chords are equidistant from the centre.
  7. Chords equidistant from the centre are equal.
  8. The longer chord is closer to the centre.
  9. The angle subtended by an arc at the centre is twice the angle subtended at the circle.
  10. The angle in a semicircle is 90°.
  11. Opposite angles of a cyclic quadrilateral add up to 180°.

✏️ Solved Examples

Example 1

A circle has radius 5 cm. Find its diameter.

Show Solution

Diameter = 2 × Radius

= 2 × 5

✅ Diameter = 10 cm

Example 2

An arc subtends an angle of 80° at the centre. Find the angle subtended by the same arc at the circle.

Show Solution

Angle at centre = 2 × angle at circle

80° = 2 × angle

Angle = 40°

✅ Answer = 40°

Example 3

One angle of a cyclic quadrilateral is 110°. Find its opposite angle.

Show Solution

Opposite angles = 180°

Opposite angle = 180° - 110°

✅ Answer = 70°
