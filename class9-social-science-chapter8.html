📖 Chapter Overview

Economics studies how people, households, businesses and governments make choices about the use of limited resources.

Since resources are limited while human wants can be many, every choice involves selecting one option over another.

🌟 What is Economics?

Economics is the study of how people make choices when resources are scarce and have alternative uses.

Economic decisions are a part of everyday life.

⚠️ Scarcity

Scarcity means that available resources are limited compared with the wants and needs that people may have.

  • Time is limited.
  • Money is limited.
  • Natural resources are limited.
  • Human effort and skills are limited.

🤔 Choice

Because resources are scarce, people must make choices. Choosing one option often means giving up another option.

Choice is therefore one of the basic ideas of economics.

🔄 Opportunity Cost

Opportunity cost is the value of the next best alternative that is given up when a choice is made.

For example, if you spend an hour studying instead of playing a game, the opportunity cost is the value of the next best activity you gave up.

📚 Resources

Resources are inputs used to produce goods and services or satisfy human wants.

  • Land and natural resources
  • Labour
  • Capital
  • Entrepreneurship
  • Knowledge and skills

🌱 Natural Resources

Natural resources include land, water, forests, minerals, energy resources and other resources provided by nature.

Their availability and use influence economic activity.

👷 Labour

Labour refers to the physical and mental effort people use in producing goods and services.

Human skills, knowledge and experience can increase the productivity of labour.

🏭 Capital

Capital includes produced resources used to produce other goods and services.

  • Machines
  • Tools
  • Buildings
  • Equipment

👨‍💼 Entrepreneurship

Entrepreneurs organise resources, make decisions and take risks in the process of producing goods and services.

🏠 Household Choices

Households make choices about spending, saving, education, food, housing and the use of their time.

These choices are influenced by income, preferences, prices and available alternatives.

🏢 Business Choices

Businesses decide what to produce, how much to produce, which resources to use and how to organise production.

Businesses must consider costs, prices, demand and available resources.

🏛️ Government Choices

Governments also face choices because public resources are limited.

Governments decide how resources should be used for areas such as education, healthcare, infrastructure and public services.

⚖️ Trade-offs

A trade-off occurs when choosing one option means giving up some other option.

Trade-offs are a natural consequence of scarcity.

📈 Production Possibilities

An economy has limited resources and therefore cannot produce unlimited quantities of every good and service.

Using more resources for one activity may leave fewer resources available for another.

🧠 Rational Choice

Economic decision-making often involves comparing the expected benefits and costs of different alternatives.

A choice is considered rational when it best serves the decision-maker's objective given the available information and constraints.

⭐ Quick Revision

  • Economics studies choices under scarcity.
  • Resources are limited and can have alternative uses.
  • Scarcity makes choice necessary.
  • Opportunity cost is the next best alternative forgone.
  • Land, labour, capital and entrepreneurship are important resources.
  • Households, businesses and governments all make economic choices.
  • Trade-offs arise because resources are scarce.
  • Economic choices involve comparing alternatives.
