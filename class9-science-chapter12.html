📖 Chapter Overview

Earth is home to a huge variety of living organisms. Scientists classify organisms to study this diversity in an organised way.

In this chapter, we explore the diversity of life, similarities and differences among organisms and the importance of classification.

🌍 Biodiversity

Biodiversity refers to the variety of living organisms found on Earth.

Organisms differ in their size, shape, structure, habitat, nutrition and many other characteristics.

Examples

  • 🌳 Plants
  • 🐘 Animals
  • 🍄 Fungi
  • 🦠 Microorganisms

🔎 Why Do We Classify Organisms?

  • It makes the study of organisms easier.
  • It helps identify organisms.
  • It helps understand similarities and differences.
  • It helps understand relationships among organisms.
  • It provides an organised system for studying biodiversity.

📊 Basis of Classification

Organisms can be classified using characteristics such as:

  • Cell structure
  • Number of cells
  • Mode of nutrition
  • Body organisation
  • Presence or absence of a cell wall
  • Method of reproduction

🦠 Unicellular and Multicellular Organisms

Unicellular Organisms

Organisms made up of a single cell are called unicellular organisms.

Example: Amoeba.

Multicellular Organisms

Organisms made up of many cells are called multicellular organisms.

Example: Humans and most plants.

🌱 Major Groups of Organisms

Organisms can be grouped according to their important structural and functional characteristics.

Group Examples
Plants Mango, Grass, Fern
Animals Human, Cow, Fish
Fungi Mushroom, Yeast
Microorganisms Many bacteria and microscopic organisms

🌿 Plants

Plants generally contain chlorophyll and can make their own food through photosynthesis.

Plants have a cell wall and show different levels of structural organisation.

🐾 Animals

Animals are generally multicellular organisms that obtain food from other organisms.

Most animals do not have a cell wall and many are capable of movement from one place to another.

🍄 Fungi

Fungi are organisms such as mushrooms and yeast. They obtain nutrients from organic matter.

Many fungi reproduce through spores.

🧬 Classification and Evolution

Classification can also help us understand the evolutionary relationships between organisms.

Organisms that share important characteristics may have common evolutionary relationships.

🏷️ Scientific Naming

Scientists use standard scientific names so that an organism can be identified consistently around the world.

Scientific naming avoids confusion caused by different common names used in different regions and languages.
