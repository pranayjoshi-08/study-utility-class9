📖 Chapter Overview

Force can change the motion of an object. It can make an object start moving, stop a moving object, change its speed or change its direction.

In this chapter, we study force, balanced and unbalanced forces, friction and Newton's three laws of motion.

💪 The Concept of Force

A force is a push or pull that can affect the motion or shape of an object.

  • Force can start motion.
  • Force can stop motion.
  • Force can change speed.
  • Force can change direction.
  • Force can change the shape of an object.

The SI unit of force is newton (N).

⚖️ Balanced and Unbalanced Forces

Balanced Forces

Forces are balanced when their net effect is zero. Balanced forces do not produce acceleration.

Unbalanced Forces

When the forces acting on an object do not balance each other, a non-zero net force acts on the object.

An unbalanced force can change the velocity of an object.

🧮 Net Force

Net force is the overall force acting on an object after considering all the forces acting on it.

Same Direction

If two forces act in the same direction:

Net Force = F₁ + F₂

Opposite Directions

If two forces act in opposite directions, the net force is the difference between their magnitudes and acts in the direction of the larger force.

🛞 Force of Friction

Friction is a force that opposes the relative motion or tendency of motion between surfaces in contact.

  • Friction acts opposite to the direction of motion.
  • It can slow down moving objects.
  • It can prevent an object from starting to move.
  • Its magnitude depends on the nature of surfaces in contact.

Examples

  • 🚲 Bicycle tyres grip the road because of friction.
  • 👟 Friction helps us walk without slipping.
  • ⚽ A rolling ball eventually slows down because of friction.

🧠 Inertia

Inertia is the tendency of an object to resist a change in its state of rest or uniform motion.

Greater mass means greater inertia.

1️⃣ Newton's First Law of Motion

An object at rest remains at rest, and an object in motion continues to move with constant velocity unless a net force acts on it.

This law is also called the law of inertia.

Example

When a moving bus stops suddenly, passengers tend to move forward because their bodies tend to maintain their state of motion.

2️⃣ Newton's Second Law of Motion

Newton's second law relates the net force acting on an object to its acceleration and mass.

F = ma

  • F = net force
  • m = mass of the object
  • a = acceleration

Example

If a 5 kg object experiences an acceleration of 2 m/s²:

F = 5 × 2 = 10 N

3️⃣ Newton's Third Law of Motion

When two objects interact, they exert forces on each other that are equal in magnitude and opposite in direction.

These forces act on different objects.

Examples

  • 🚶 Walking involves interaction between our feet and the ground.
  • 🏊 Swimming involves pushing water backward.
  • 🚣 A boat moves forward when water is pushed backward.

📊 Types of Forces

⭐ Quick Revision

  • Force is a push or pull.
  • The SI unit of force is newton (N).
  • Balanced forces have zero net force.
  • Unbalanced forces produce a non-zero net force.
  • Friction opposes relative motion.
  • Inertia is resistance to change in motion.
  • Newton's first law explains inertia.
  • Newton's second law gives F = ma.
  • Newton's third law describes interaction force pairs.
