📖 Chapter Overview

Work and energy are closely connected with the motion and functioning of objects around us.

In this chapter, we explore mechanical work, energy, power and simple machines that make everyday tasks easier.

💪 What is Work?

In science, work is done when a force acting on an object produces displacement in the direction of the force.

Work = Force × Displacement

The SI unit of work is joule (J).

Example

If a force of 10 N moves an object through 2 m in the direction of the force:

Work = 10 × 2 = 20 J

🚫 When is Work Zero?

Work is zero when there is no displacement of the object.

Work can also be zero when the force is perpendicular to the displacement.

Examples

  • Holding a heavy object stationary.
  • Pushing a wall that does not move.
  • Carrying an object horizontally at constant height, when considering the upward force due to gravity.

⚡ Energy

Energy is the capacity of an object or system to do work or bring about changes.

The SI unit of energy is joule (J).

🏃 Kinetic Energy

Kinetic energy is the energy possessed by an object due to its motion.

KE = ½mv²

  • m = mass of the object
  • v = speed of the object

Example

A moving car has kinetic energy because it is in motion.

🏔️ Potential Energy

Potential energy is energy stored in an object or system because of its position, configuration or condition.

Gravitational Potential Energy

For an object near the Earth's surface:

PE = mgh

  • m = mass
  • g = acceleration due to gravity
  • h = height

Examples

  • Water stored at a height in a dam.
  • A book kept on a shelf.
  • A raised object.

🔄 Transformation of Energy

Energy can change from one form to another.

  • Electrical energy → Light energy in a lamp.
  • Electrical energy → Mechanical energy in a fan.
  • Potential energy → Kinetic energy when an object falls.
  • Chemical energy → Heat and mechanical energy in an engine.

♻️ Conservation of Energy

Energy cannot be created or destroyed. It can only be transformed from one form to another.

Therefore, the total energy of an isolated system remains constant.

⏱️ Power

Power tells us how quickly work is done or energy is transferred.

Power = Work ÷ Time

The SI unit of power is watt (W).

1 watt = 1 joule per second

⚙️ Simple Machines

Simple machines are devices that help us perform tasks more easily by changing the magnitude or direction of a force.

Examples

  • Lever
  • Pulley
  • Wheel and axle
  • Inclined plane
  • Wedge
  • Screw

🔧 Lever

A lever is a rigid bar that can turn about a fixed point called the fulcrum.

  • Fulcrum: Fixed point about which the lever turns.
  • Load: The object or resistance to be moved.
  • Effort: The force applied to the lever.

Examples

  • Seesaw
  • Scissors
  • Bottle opener
  • Crowbar

🪢 Pulley

A pulley is a wheel with a groove around its rim through which a rope or cable can pass.

Pulleys can change the direction of an applied force and, in suitable arrangements, provide mechanical advantage.

Example

A pulley system can be used for lifting loads.

⛰️ Inclined Plane

An inclined plane is a sloping surface used to raise or lower objects.

It allows a load to be moved through a greater distance using a smaller force than lifting it directly.

Examples

  • Ramps
  • Sloping roads
  • Slides

🔩 Screw and Wedge

Screw

A screw is an inclined plane wrapped around a cylinder. It is useful for fastening objects and lifting or moving certain loads.

Wedge

A wedge is commonly used for cutting, splitting or separating materials.

  • Knife
  • Ax
  • Chisel

  • Kinetic energy is due to motion.
  • Potential energy is stored energy due to position or configuration.
  • Energy can change from one form to another.
  • Energy is conserved.
  • Power is the rate of doing work.
