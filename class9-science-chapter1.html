🌍 What is Science?

Science is a systematic way of understanding the natural world through observation, questioning, evidence, experimentation and reasoning.

Science begins with curiosity. We observe something, ask questions about it, collect evidence and use reasoning to understand what is happening.

🔎 Scientific Exploration

Scientific exploration is a purposeful process of observing the world, asking meaningful questions, testing ideas and using evidence to reach conclusions.

Basic Steps

  1. Observe a phenomenon.
  2. Ask a question.
  3. Suggest an explanation or model.
  4. Make a prediction.
  5. Test the prediction.
  6. Collect and analyse evidence.
  7. Revise the idea when necessary.

🧠 Observation and Questions

Careful observation is an important starting point of scientific investigation.

A good scientific question should be clear and possible to investigate using observations or experiments.

Example

Observation: Ice melts faster in warm water.

Scientific question: How does water temperature affect the rate at which ice melts?

📦 Models in Science

The natural world is very complex. Scientists often use models to represent important features of real systems.

A model is a simplified representation of a real system. It focuses on the details that are important for a particular question.

Examples

  • A moving car may be represented as a point in physics.
  • Atoms may be represented using spheres and bonds.
  • Cells may be represented using labelled diagrams.
  • The Earth may be represented as a sphere with layers.

⚙️ Assumptions in Models

Scientists deliberately ignore some details when creating a simple model.

For example, while studying a falling object, air resistance may be ignored in a simple model so that the effect of gravity can be studied more easily.

As a model becomes more detailed, additional factors can be included to improve its accuracy.

📏 Measurement

Measurement means assigning numerical values to observations using standard units.

Standard measurements allow scientists to compare results accurately.

Common SI Units

Quantity SI Unit Symbol
Length metre m
Mass kilogram kg
Time second s
Temperature kelvin K
Electric current ampere A

🎯 Accuracy, Uncertainty and Estimation

Measurements are not always perfectly exact. Every measurement has some degree of uncertainty.

Estimation is useful when an exact measurement is difficult or when we only need an approximate value.

Example

Instead of measuring the exact number of people in a large crowd one by one, we may estimate the number using available information.

🔢 Mathematics in Science

Mathematics helps scientists describe patterns, relationships and measurements precisely.

Scientific ideas can often be represented using numbers, symbols, equations, tables and graphs.

Example

If a car travels 100 metres in 10 seconds, its average speed can be calculated as:

Speed = Distance ÷ Time

Speed = 100 ÷ 10 = 10 m/s

🗣️ Scientific Language and Symbols

Scientific terms often have precise meanings. Scientists use common symbols, units and terminology so that information can be communicated clearly.

📜 Laws, Theories and Principles

Scientific Law

A scientific law describes a regular pattern or relationship observed in nature.

Scientific Theory

A scientific theory is a well-supported explanation of natural phenomena based on evidence gathered over time.

Scientific Principle

A principle is a broad idea that helps us understand or describe how a system behaves.

Scientific knowledge can be improved or revised when new evidence becomes available.

🔮 Predictions

Scientific predictions are based on evidence, observations, reasoning and models rather than simple guesses.

Example

If repeated observations show that a plant grows faster when it receives sufficient light, we can make a prediction about its growth under similar conditions.

🔬 Designing a Fair Experiment

A fair experiment changes one important factor while keeping other relevant conditions controlled.

Important Features

  • Clear question
  • Suitable variables
  • Controlled conditions
  • Careful measurements
  • Reliable observations
  • Evidence-based conclusion

🌐 Branches of Science

  • ⚛️ Physics - matter, motion, energy and forces
  • 🧪 Chemistry - substances and their changes
  • 🧬 Biology - living organisms and life processes
  • 🌍 Earth Science - Earth and its systems

These branches are connected and often work together to understand complex natural phenomena.

⭐ Quick Revision

  • Science begins with curiosity and observation.
  • Scientific questions should be testable.
  • Models simplify complex real-world systems.
  • Measurements use standard units.
  • SI units provide a common measurement system.
  • Mathematics helps express scientific relationships.
  • Evidence is important for scientific conclusions.
  • Scientific ideas can be revised with new evidence.
  • Predictions should be based on evidence and reasoning.
