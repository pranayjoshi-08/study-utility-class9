⚛️ Journey Inside the Atom

Class 9 Science

📘 New NCERT - Exploration

📖 Chapter Overview

Everything around us is made up of matter. Matter is made of tiny particles called atoms.

In this chapter, we explore the structure of an atom, its subatomic particles and the development of different atomic models.

⚛️ What is an Atom?

An atom is the smallest unit of an element that retains the chemical identity of that element.

Atoms are extremely small and contain smaller particles called subatomic particles.

🔬 Subatomic Particles

Particle Charge Location
Electron Negative (-) Outside the nucleus
Proton Positive (+) Nucleus
Neutron No charge Nucleus

⚡ Electron

Electrons are negatively charged subatomic particles. They are found outside the nucleus in regions associated with different energy levels.

The charge of an electron is negative.

➕ Proton

Protons are positively charged particles found inside the nucleus of an atom.

The number of protons determines the atomic number of an element.

⚪ Neutron

Neutrons are electrically neutral particles found inside the nucleus.

Protons and neutrons together make up most of the mass of an atom.

🎯 The Nucleus

The nucleus is the small, dense central region of an atom.

  • It contains protons and neutrons.
  • It carries an overall positive charge.
  • Most of the mass of an atom is concentrated in the nucleus.

🔎 Discovery of the Electron

J. J. Thomson carried out experiments with cathode rays and showed that atoms contain negatively charged particles.

These particles were later called electrons.

🥧 Thomson's Atomic Model

J. J. Thomson proposed a model in which the atom was considered to contain positive charge with electrons embedded in it.

This model is often described as the plum-pudding model.

🎯 Rutherford's Experiment

Ernest Rutherford and his collaborators performed the alpha-particle scattering experiment using a very thin metal foil.

Most alpha particles passed through the foil, while a small number were deflected and a very few were reflected back.

Conclusions

  • Most of the atom is empty space.
  • Positive charge is concentrated in a very small region.
  • The nucleus is extremely small compared with the atom.
  • Most of the mass is concentrated in the nucleus.

🪐 Rutherford's Atomic Model

Rutherford proposed that an atom has a tiny positively charged nucleus at its centre, with electrons moving around it.

However, this model could not explain why electrons do not continuously lose energy and fall into the nucleus.

🌟 Bohr's Atomic Model

Niels Bohr proposed that electrons move around the nucleus only in certain permitted energy levels or shells.

  • Electrons occupy specific energy levels.
  • Electrons do not continuously lose energy while occupying a permitted energy level.
  • An electron can move between energy levels by gaining or losing a definite amount of energy.

🔵 Electron Shells

Electron shells are represented by letters such as K, L, M and N.

The shell closest to the nucleus has the lowest energy. Energy generally increases as the distance from the nucleus increases.

Maximum Electron Capacity

The maximum number of electrons that a shell can hold is given by:

2n²

Here, n represents the shell number.

🧮 Atomic Number

The atomic number of an element is the number of protons present in the nucleus of its atom.

Atomic Number = Number of Protons

For a neutral atom:

Number of Protons = Number of Electrons

⚖️ Mass Number

The mass number of an atom is the total number of protons and neutrons present in its nucleus.

Mass Number = Protons + Neutrons

🔢 Isotopes

Isotopes are atoms of the same element having the same atomic number but different mass numbers.

Example

Hydrogen has three commonly discussed isotopes:

  • Protium
  • Deuterium
  • Tritium

🔄 Isobars

Isobars are atoms of different elements having the same mass number but different atomic numbers.

📊 Atomic Structure at a Glance

Term Meaning
Atomic Number Number of protons
Mass Number Number of protons + neutrons
Isotopes Same atomic number, different mass numbers
Isobars Same mass number, different atomic numbers

⭐ Quick Revision

  • An atom contains electrons, protons and neutrons.
  • Electrons have negative charge.
  • Protons have positive charge.
  • Neutrons have no electric charge.
  • The nucleus contains protons and neutrons.
  • Most of the atom is empty space.
  • Bohr proposed definite energy levels for electrons.
  • Atomic number equals the number of protons.
  • Mass number equals protons plus neutrons.
  • Isotopes have the same atomic number but different mass numbers.
