🔬 Cell: The Building Block of Life

Class 9 Science

📘 New NCERT - Exploration

📖 Chapter Overview

The cell is the basic structural and functional unit of life. All living organisms are made up of cells.

Cells differ in their shape, size and functions, but they share several basic features.

🧫 What is a Cell?

A cell is the smallest unit of an organism that can carry out the basic activities necessary for life.

Some organisms consist of only one cell, while others are made up of many cells.

Examples

  • 🦠 Bacteria - usually single-celled organisms
  • 🦠 Amoeba - single-celled organism
  • 🌱 Plants - multicellular organisms
  • 🐘 Animals - multicellular organisms

🔬 Unicellular and Multicellular Organisms

Unicellular Organisms

Organisms made up of a single cell are called unicellular organisms.

Examples: Amoeba, Paramecium and many bacteria.

Multicellular Organisms

Organisms made up of many cells are called multicellular organisms.

Examples: humans, plants, dogs and birds.

🌱 Plant Cell and 🐾 Animal Cell

Plant and animal cells have many common structures, but plant cells also have some structures that animal cells do not have.

Feature Plant Cell Animal Cell
Cell membrane Present Present
Cell wall Present Absent
Chloroplasts Usually present in photosynthetic cells Absent
Nucleus Present in typical cells Present

🧱 Cell Wall

The cell wall is a rigid outer layer found outside the cell membrane in plant cells.

It provides support, protection and helps the plant cell maintain its shape.

🫧 Cell Membrane

The cell membrane forms the boundary of the cell.

It controls the movement of many substances into and out of the cell.

It is also called the plasma membrane.

🧠 Nucleus

The nucleus contains genetic material and plays an important role in controlling the activities of the cell.

The genetic material carries information needed for growth, development and reproduction.

⚡ Cytoplasm

Cytoplasm is the material present inside the cell membrane and outside the nucleus in cells that have a nucleus.

Many important chemical reactions of the cell occur in the cytoplasm.

🔋 Mitochondria

Mitochondria are structures involved in releasing usable energy from food molecules.

They are often called the powerhouses of the cell.

🌿 Chloroplasts

Chloroplasts are found in plant cells and contain chlorophyll.

They are the sites where photosynthesis takes place in photosynthetic plant cells.

💧 Vacuoles

Vacuoles are membrane-bound structures that can store water, nutrients and other substances.

Plant cells often have a large central vacuole.

📦 Cell Organelles

Different structures inside cells perform different functions. These specialised structures are commonly called cell organelles.

Structure Main Function
Nucleus Contains genetic material and regulates cell activities
Mitochondria Helps release usable energy
Chloroplast Site of photosynthesis
Vacuole Storage of various substances
Cell membrane Controls movement of substances

🔍 Looking at Cells

Cells are usually too small to be seen clearly with the naked eye. A microscope is used to observe cells and their structures.

Microscopes have played an important role in the development of cell biology.

🧪 Cell Size and Shape

Cells can have different sizes and shapes depending on their functions.

For example, nerve cells have a shape suited to transmitting signals over distances, while red blood cells have a shape suited to their role in transporting oxygen.

🔄 Cell Division

New cells are produced from existing cells through processes of cell division.

Cell division is important for growth, repair and reproduction.

⭐ Why are Cells Important?

  • Cells form the basic structure of living organisms.
  • Cells carry out essential life processes.
  • Cells obtain and use energy.
  • Cells respond to their surroundings.
  • Cells grow and divide.
  • Cells contain genetic information.
