💹 The Price Puzzle: What Drives the Market

📖 Chapter Overview

Markets are an important part of economic life. They connect buyers and sellers and help determine the prices at which goods and services are exchanged.

Prices are influenced by several factors, especially demand, supply, costs, competition and the choices of buyers and sellers.

🏪 What is a Market?

A market is a system or arrangement through which buyers and sellers interact to exchange goods and services.

A market does not always have to be a physical place. Buying and selling can also take place through digital platforms and other forms of communication.

👨‍👩‍👧 Buyers and Sellers

Buyers demand goods and services because they want to satisfy their needs and wants.

Sellers supply goods and services in the hope of receiving payment in return.

📈 Demand

Demand refers to the quantity of a good or service that buyers are willing and able to purchase at different prices.

Generally, when the price of a good rises, buyers tend to purchase less of it, other things remaining unchanged.

📉 Supply

Supply refers to the quantity of a good or service that sellers are willing and able to offer for sale at different prices.

Generally, a higher price can encourage producers to supply more, other things remaining unchanged.

⚖️ Demand and Supply

The interaction between demand and supply helps determine the price and quantity traded in a market.

If demand changes while supply remains unchanged, prices may change. Similarly, changes in supply can affect prices.

💰 Price

Price is the amount of money paid for a good or service.

Prices provide information to buyers and sellers and influence their economic decisions.

🔄 Change in Demand

Demand can change because of factors other than the price of the good itself.

  • Income of consumers
  • Preferences and tastes
  • Prices of related goods
  • Expectations about future prices
  • Number of consumers

🏭 Change in Supply

Supply can change because of factors affecting producers and production.

  • Cost of inputs
  • Technology
  • Taxes and regulations
  • Weather and natural conditions
  • Number of producers

🥭 Example: Vegetable Market

Suppose heavy rainfall damages a vegetable crop. The amount available for sale may fall.

If demand remains similar while supply decreases, the market price may rise.

🛒 Example: Festival Demand

During festivals, demand for certain goods may increase. If sellers cannot immediately increase supply, prices may rise.

This shows how changes in demand can influence market prices.

🏆 Competition

Competition exists when several sellers offer similar goods or services to buyers.

Competition can encourage sellers to improve quality, offer better prices and respond to consumer preferences.

🧑‍💼 Producers and Costs

Producers have to pay for inputs such as labour, raw materials, energy, transport and equipment.

Changes in production costs can influence the prices at which producers are willing to sell.

🧠 Consumer Choices

Consumers make choices based on their needs, preferences, income and the prices of available alternatives.

These individual choices together influence demand in markets.

🌐 Markets in Everyday Life

Markets are present in many parts of daily life, including food, clothing, transport, education, entertainment and digital services.

Modern technology has also created online markets where buyers and sellers can interact over long distances.

⚠️ When Markets Change

Markets can change because of natural events, technological developments, government policies, changes in income and changes in consumer preferences.

Understanding these changes helps us understand why prices do not remain fixed forever.

⭐ Quick Revision

  • A market connects buyers and sellers.
  • Demand represents buyers' willingness and ability to purchase.
  • Supply represents sellers' willingness and ability to sell.
  • The interaction of demand and supply influences market prices.
  • Changes in income and preferences can affect demand.
  • Changes in production costs and technology can affect supply.
  • Competition influences sellers' decisions.
  • Prices provide information and incentives to market participants.
  • Markets can be physical or online.
