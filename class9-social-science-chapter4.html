📖 Chapter Overview

Human history began long before written records existed. Early humans gradually developed tools, learned to use fire, adapted to different environments and developed new ways of living.

Over a very long period, humans moved from hunting and gathering towards farming, settled communities and eventually early civilisations.

🧬 Human Evolution

Human evolution was a long process involving changes in physical features, behaviour and ways of living.

Modern humans belong to the species Homo sapiens.

  • Early human ancestors gradually developed new abilities.
  • Tool-making became increasingly sophisticated.
  • Humans adapted to different environments.
  • Cooperation helped groups survive.

🪨 Stone Age

The Stone Age refers broadly to a long period when stone was an important material for making tools.

Important Periods

  • Palaeolithic Age – Old Stone Age
  • Mesolithic Age – Middle Stone Age
  • Neolithic Age – New Stone Age

🏹 Hunter-Gatherers

Early humans depended mainly on hunting animals, fishing, collecting fruits, roots, seeds and other edible resources.

They often moved from place to place in search of food and suitable shelter.

🔥 Discovery and Use of Fire

Fire became an important part of human life. It provided warmth and light and could be used for cooking and protection.

The controlled use of fire was an important development in human history.

🛠️ Development of Tools

Early humans made tools from materials such as stone, bone and wood.

Tool-making techniques became more advanced over time.

  • Cutting tools
  • Scrapers
  • Hand axes
  • Arrowheads
  • Other specialised tools

🎨 Art and Culture

Early humans created paintings, engravings and other forms of symbolic expression.

Rock art provides valuable evidence about aspects of early human life, beliefs and activities.

🌾 Beginning of Agriculture

One of the major changes in human history was the development of agriculture.

People began growing crops and domesticating animals. This gradually encouraged more settled ways of life.

🏘️ Settled Communities

Farming and animal domestication allowed people to remain in one place for longer periods.

Permanent or semi-permanent settlements developed, and communities became larger and more organised.

🔨 Chalcolithic and Bronze Age

Later communities began using metals along with stone. Copper was one of the earliest metals widely used.

The development of bronze technology supported the growth of more complex societies.

🏙️ Beginning of Civilisation

As settlements grew, some societies developed cities, specialised occupations, systems of administration, trade and writing.

These developments marked important steps towards civilisation.

🌏 Early Civilisations

Important early civilisations developed in different parts of the world.

  • 🌊 Sindhu–Sarasvatī civilisation
  • 🏺 Mesopotamian civilisation
  • 🇪🇬 Egyptian civilisation
  • 🐉 Chinese civilisation

🏛️ Features of Early Civilisations

  • Urban settlements
  • Specialised occupations
  • Long-distance trade
  • Organised administration
  • Technological development
  • Systems of writing or record-keeping
  • Social organisation

🇮🇳 Sindhu–Sarasvatī Civilisation

The Sindhu–Sarasvatī civilisation developed in parts of the north-western Indian subcontinent.

Archaeological evidence shows planned settlements, craft production, trade networks and sophisticated systems of water management.

📜 Writing and Records

The development of writing and record-keeping was an important milestone because it allowed information to be recorded and transmitted across generations.

⭐ Quick Revision

  • Modern humans are called Homo sapiens.
  • Early humans used stone, bone and wood to make tools.
  • Hunter-gatherers depended on naturally available resources.
  • Fire became an important part of human life.
  • Agriculture encouraged settled communities.
  • Animal domestication was an important development.
  • Metalworking developed after long periods of stone-tool use.
  • Growing settlements eventually developed into complex societies.
  • Early civilisations developed in several parts of the world.
  • Archaeological evidence helps us study prehistoric societies.
