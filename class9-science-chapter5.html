🧪 Exploring Mixtures and their Separation

Class 9 Science

📘 New NCERT - Exploration

📖 Chapter Overview

Mixtures are found everywhere around us. Air, soil, milk, seawater and many foods are examples of mixtures.

This chapter explores different types of mixtures, their properties, concentration and methods used to separate their components.

🧪 What is a Mixture?

A mixture is formed when two or more substances are combined physically without forming a new substance through a chemical reaction.

Examples

  • Salt and water
  • Sand and water
  • Air
  • Soil
  • Oil and water

🔬 Homogeneous and Heterogeneous Mixtures

Homogeneous Mixture

A homogeneous mixture has a uniform composition throughout.

Example: salt dissolved completely in water.

Heterogeneous Mixture

A heterogeneous mixture does not have a uniform composition throughout.

Example: sand mixed with water.

🥤 Solutions

A solution is a homogeneous mixture in which one or more substances are uniformly distributed in another substance.

Solute

The substance that gets dissolved is called the solute.

Solvent

The substance in which the solute dissolves is called the solvent.

Example

In a salt-water solution:

  • Salt = Solute
  • Water = Solvent

📊 Concentration of a Solution

The concentration tells us how much solute is present in a given amount of solution or solvent.

Mass by Mass Percentage

Mass % = (Mass of solute ÷ Mass of solution) × 100

Mass by Volume Percentage

Mass/Volume % = (Mass of solute ÷ Volume of solution) × 100

Volume by Volume Percentage

Volume/Volume % = (Volume of solute ÷ Volume of solution) × 100

💧 Solubility

Solubility is the ability of a substance to dissolve in a particular solvent under specified conditions.

The amount of a substance that can dissolve may depend on factors such as temperature and the nature of the substances involved.

🌫️ Suspensions

A suspension is a heterogeneous mixture in which insoluble particles are dispersed in a fluid.

  • The particles are large enough to be observed.
  • They may settle down when left undisturbed.
  • They can generally be separated by filtration.

Example: muddy water.

☁️ Colloids

A colloid contains very small particles dispersed in another substance.

The particles are generally not visible to the naked eye, but they can scatter light.

Examples

  • Milk
  • Fog
  • Smoke
  • Some paints

💡 Tyndall Effect

The scattering of light by particles in a colloid is called the Tyndall effect.

It can make the path of a beam of light visible when the light passes through certain colloidal mixtures.

🔄 Methods of Separation

Different mixtures require different separation methods. The method chosen depends on the properties of their components.

Method Used For
Filtration Separating insoluble solids from liquids
Evaporation Obtaining a dissolved solid from a solution
Crystallisation Obtaining crystals of a dissolved substance
Distillation Separating liquids based on differences in boiling points
Chromatography Separating components of a mixture based on their movement through a medium
Sublimation Separating a sublimable solid from suitable mixtures
Centrifugation Separating components using rapid spinning
Separating Funnel Separating immiscible liquids

💎 Crystallisation

Crystallisation is a method used to obtain relatively pure crystals of a substance from a solution.

It is useful when a solid is dissolved in a liquid and needs to be recovered in crystalline form.

🔥 Distillation

Distillation can be used to separate liquids based on differences in their boiling points, or to obtain a liquid from a solution.

It involves vaporisation followed by condensation.

🧻 Paper Chromatography

Paper chromatography is a technique used to separate different components of a mixture.

Different substances travel different distances through the paper because they interact differently with the paper and the moving solvent.

🌀 Centrifugation

Centrifugation separates components of a mixture by spinning it rapidly.

It is useful when particles have different densities or when very small suspended particles need to be separated.

🔻 Separating Funnel

A separating funnel is used to separate immiscible liquids that form separate layers.

Example: oil and water.

⭐ Quick Revision

  • A mixture contains two or more physically combined substances.
  • Homogeneous mixtures have uniform composition.
  • Heterogeneous mixtures have non-uniform composition.
  • A solution contains a solute and a solvent.
  • Suspensions contain relatively large dispersed particles.
  • Colloids can show the Tyndall effect.
  • Filtration can separate suitable insoluble solids from liquids.
  • Crystallisation can produce crystals from solutions.
  • Distillation involves vaporisation and condensation.
  • Chromatography can separate components of mixtures.
  • Centrifugation uses rapid spinning for separation.
