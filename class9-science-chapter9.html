🧪 Atomic Foundations of Matter

Class 9 Science

📘 New NCERT - Exploration

📖 Chapter Overview

Matter is made up of atoms and molecules. Understanding atoms, molecules and their combinations helps us understand the composition of different substances.

In this chapter, we explore elements, compounds, molecules, chemical formulae and the basic ideas behind chemical combinations.

⚛️ Atoms and Molecules

An atom is the smallest unit of an element that retains the chemical identity of that element.

A molecule is a group of two or more atoms chemically bonded together and capable of independent existence.

Examples

  • H₂ — Hydrogen molecule
  • O₂ — Oxygen molecule
  • H₂O — Water molecule
  • CO₂ — Carbon dioxide molecule

🧪 Elements

An element is a pure substance made up of only one kind of atom.

Examples

  • Hydrogen
  • Oxygen
  • Carbon
  • Iron
  • Copper
  • Gold

🔗 Compounds

A compound is a pure substance formed when two or more different elements combine chemically in a fixed ratio.

Examples

  • H₂O — Water
  • CO₂ — Carbon dioxide
  • NaCl — Sodium chloride

📜 Laws of Chemical Combination

1. Law of Conservation of Mass

In a chemical reaction, mass is neither created nor destroyed. The total mass of the reactants is equal to the total mass of the products.

2. Law of Constant Proportions

A pure compound always contains the same elements combined in the same fixed proportion by mass.

🔢 Atomic Mass

Atomic mass represents the relative mass of an atom compared with a standard reference.

Atomic masses are commonly expressed in atomic mass units, represented by u.

⚖️ Molecular Mass

Molecular mass is the sum of the atomic masses of all the atoms present in one molecule.

Example: Water (H₂O)

H = 1 u and O = 16 u

Molecular mass of H₂O = 2 × 1 + 16 = 18 u

🧮 Formula Unit

Ionic compounds do not normally exist as separate molecules. Their composition is represented by a formula unit.

Example

NaCl represents sodium chloride in terms of its simplest ratio of sodium and chloride ions.

🔬 Chemical Formula

A chemical formula represents the elements present in a substance and the relative number of their atoms or ions.

Substance Formula
Water H₂O
Carbon dioxide CO₂
Ammonia NH₃
Methane CH₄
Sodium chloride NaCl

🧠 Valency

Valency describes the combining capacity of an atom.

It helps us determine the chemical formula of compounds.

Examples

  • Hydrogen — Valency 1
  • Oxygen — Valency 2
  • Nitrogen — Valency 3
  • Carbon — Valency 4

📐 Writing Chemical Formulae

The valencies of the combining elements are used to determine the simplest whole-number ratio of their atoms or ions.

Example: Magnesium Chloride

Magnesium has valency 2 and chlorine has valency 1.

Therefore, the formula is:

MgCl₂

🌟 Mole Concept

A mole is a convenient unit for counting extremely large numbers of particles such as atoms, molecules or ions.

One mole contains approximately 6.022 × 10²³ particles.

This number is called the Avogadro constant.

📦 Molar Mass

The molar mass of a substance is the mass of one mole of that substance.

Its SI-compatible commonly used unit is g/mol.

Example

The molar mass of water (H₂O) is approximately 18 g/mol.
