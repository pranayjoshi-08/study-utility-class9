🚗 Describing Motion Around Us

Class 9 Science

📘 New NCERT - Exploration

```

📖 Chapter Overview

Motion is a change in the position of an object with time relative to a chosen reference point.

We see motion everywhere around us — vehicles move on roads, birds fly, planets move around the Sun and people walk from one place to another.

📍 Position and Reference Point

To describe the position or motion of an object, we need a reference point or reference frame.

The position of an object tells us where it is with respect to the chosen reference point.

Example

If a student is sitting inside a moving bus, the student is at rest relative to the bus but is moving relative to a person standing on the road.

🏃 Motion

An object is said to be in motion when its position changes with time relative to a reference point.

An object is at rest when its position does not change with time relative to the reference point.

📏 Distance

Distance is the total length of the path travelled by an object.

  • Distance is a scalar quantity.
  • It has magnitude but no direction.
  • Its SI unit is metre (m).
  • Distance is always non-negative.

Example

If a student walks 50 m to school and then walks another 20 m, the total distance travelled is:

Distance = 50 + 20 = 70 m

➡️ Displacement

Displacement describes the change in position of an object from its initial position to its final position.

  • Displacement has both magnitude and direction.
  • It is a vector quantity.
  • Its SI unit is metre (m).
  • Its magnitude can be zero even when distance is not zero.

Example

If a person walks around a track and returns to the starting point, the total distance is not zero, but the displacement is zero.

⚖️ Distance vs Displacement

Distance Displacement
Total path travelled Change from initial to final position
Scalar quantity Vector quantity
Has magnitude only Has magnitude and direction
Cannot be negative Can have positive, negative or zero value depending on direction

⚡ Speed

Speed tells us how quickly an object covers distance.

Speed = Distance ÷ Time

The SI unit of speed is metre per second (m/s).

Example

If a car travels 120 m in 10 s:

Speed = 120 ÷ 10 = 12 m/s

🏎️ Average Speed

Average speed is the total distance travelled divided by the total time taken.

Average Speed = Total Distance ÷ Total Time

Example

A cyclist travels 200 m in 20 s and then 300 m in 30 s.

Total distance = 500 m

Total time = 50 s

Average speed = 500 ÷ 50 = 10 m/s

➡️ Velocity

Velocity describes the rate of change of displacement with time.

Velocity = Displacement ÷ Time

Velocity is a vector quantity, so its direction is important.

Its SI unit is m/s.

🚦 Uniform and Non-uniform Motion

Uniform Motion

An object is in uniform motion when it covers equal distances in equal intervals of time.

Non-uniform Motion

An object is in non-uniform motion when it covers unequal distances in equal intervals of time, or its direction changes with time.

📈 Motion Through Graphs

Graphs help us represent and understand the motion of objects.

Distance-Time Graph

A distance-time graph shows how the distance travelled changes with time.

  • A horizontal line represents no change in position.
  • A straight sloping line can represent uniform motion.
  • A changing slope indicates changing speed.

📊 Velocity-Time Graph

A velocity-time graph shows how velocity changes with time.

  • A horizontal line indicates constant velocity.
  • An increasing velocity indicates acceleration.
  • A decreasing velocity indicates slowing down.

🚀 Acceleration

Acceleration describes how quickly velocity changes with time.

Acceleration = Change in Velocity ÷ Time

The SI unit of acceleration is m/s².

Example

If the velocity of a vehicle changes from 5 m/s to 15 m/s in 5 seconds:

Acceleration = (15 - 5) ÷ 5 = 2 m/s²

🔄 Circular Motion

When an object moves along a circular path, its direction changes continuously.

Even if the speed remains constant, the velocity changes because velocity includes direction.

Examples

  • 🚲 A point on a rotating bicycle wheel
  • 🎡 A person moving on a Ferris wheel
  • 🌍 Motion of objects in circular paths

⭐ Quick Revision

  • Motion is described relative to a reference point.
  • Distance is the total path travelled.
  • Displacement is the change in position.
  • Speed is distance travelled per unit time.
  • Velocity is displacement per unit time.
  • Acceleration is the change in velocity per unit time.
  • Distance is scalar; displacement is vector.
  • Speed is scalar; velocity is vector.
  • Graphs help us study motion.
  • Direction changes can change velocity even when speed remains constant.
```
